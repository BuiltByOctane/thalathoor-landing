The house

A home, kept as a home.

The house has been with the family for three generations. Its compound once held a school, where Dr. Nair's great-grandfather taught the village children — and the walls still carry those stories. The rooms are kept much as they were, filled with local handicrafts and flowers cut from the surrounding gardens.

It is run by Shyamala Nair, one of the pioneers of hospitality in north Kerala. travelers and neighbours were welcomed into her kitchen and served a meal, reflecting the timeless tradition that anyone who arrived during mealtime was invited to share the family's table. This cherished custom continues to this day, with every guest dining alongside the family, not as a visitor, but as one of their own.

Nothing here has been turned into something else. The architecture, the courtyard, the kitchen — all kept, not restored. Mornings are quiet. Evenings are quieter.

The kitchen

The recipes nobody writes down.

Kasaragod cooks differently from the rest of Kerala. Closer to the Tulu and Kannada border, heavier on coconut, lighter on the things that travelled south. Most of it never made it onto a restaurant menu, because it was never restaurant food it was the family traditional food.

Nothing is prepped the day before. Vegetables are what the season and the local market gave us that morning, which means the menu shifts through the year and we won't pretend otherwise.

Shyamala's home cooking is what guests remember most — and breakfast, lunch and dinner are all included in the stay. Kerala staples are here too — [[TBD-17: 2–3 dishes]] — but the dish people write to us about afterwards is usually something they hadn't eaten since childhood, and couldn't name.

The natural setting

Paddy fields, a river, and time to spare.

The house sits beside open paddy fields, across an arecanut plantation, with views that open toward the river. Within the property is a natural pond filled with clear rainwater, free from chlorine, with its waters flowing gently toward the river. The pond is perfect for a refreshing dip or swim on a warm afternoon..

Afterwards, enjoy a cup of hot tea and freshly prepared snacks on the verandah, overlooking the paddy fields, arecanut trees and river beyond.
